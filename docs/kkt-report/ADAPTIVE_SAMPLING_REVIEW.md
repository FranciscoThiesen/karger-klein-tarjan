# Independent review of the prospective adaptive sampling bound

PASS, source/theory review by `multigraph_design`, 2026-10-15. No implementation, local execution, test or performance measurement was performed. This review changes no frozen experiment.

I checked the primary [Karger–Klein–Tarjan paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/karger/Papers/mst.pdf), Section 2, Lemma 1, and its possibly disconnected minimum-spanning-forest setting. Its independent-edge sampling bound supplies the proposed conditional q/p term. The extension to separately sampled parallel records follows the same increasing-weight forest argument; cycles of two parallel records do not invalidate it. The paper does not propose this adaptive policy. The potential argument below is our own derivation under the proposal's linear-primitive and fresh-independent-sample assumptions.

Conditioning on the entire post-contraction quotient and prior history makes q,r and the chosen p deterministic. The sample contributes expected pr edges and the filtered child at most q/p edges. Independence between these two child sizes is unnecessary: linearity of expectation is enough. Both children have at most q vertices, including disconnected samples before isolated-vertex deletion. Conditional induction for their future calls requires fresh independent sampling; the stated warning about finite mt19937 streams is appropriate.

Since 1/2 belongs to the candidate set, minimizing W guarantees E[X+Y | history] ≤ r/2+2q ≤ m/2+n/2. Hence local work c(m+n) plus the two inductive potentials is bounded by (c+A/2)m+(c+A/2+B/2)n. A≥2c and B≥2c+A suffice, including A=2c,B=4c. Extra selector work is covered by c; it need not equal the old implementation's measured coefficient. Empty/base overhead can be absorbed using the strictly shrinking vertex recursion and O(m+n+1) overall. This establishes the stated expected bound, not a tail bound or wall-time prediction.

For the exact selector, W(p/2)−W(p)=q/p−rp/2. It is negative exactly when rp²>2q. Once false, it stays false as p is halved, so the first non-improving step is a global dyadic minimizer; stopping at equality chooses the larger probability. At step k, t=ceil(r/4^(k−1)). The test ceil(t/8)>q is equivalent to t>8q, which is equivalent to r>8q·4^(k−1)=2q·4^k. Iterated ceiling division preserves the invariant, including exact ties and one-unit-above boundaries. Computing ceil_div as quotient plus nonzero-remainder avoids multiplication and addition-overflow hazards.

For positive q and r≤2³¹−1, k≤15 because the k=15 threshold is at least 2³¹. This is conservative when nonempty loop-free quotients have q≥2. The proposed unsigned mask shift is safe under that validated domain; neither the fixed code width nor current generator caps establish an unbounded implementation. The mathematical policy itself permits a word size sufficient for its input domain.

At root r/q=249, k=1,2,3 improve across thresholds 8,32,128, while k=4 stops at threshold512: p=1/16. As a useful hand check, W/q is126.5 at1/2,39.125 at1/8,31.5625 at1/16 and39.78125 at1/32. These are child-edge-volume upper-bound values, not observed survivors, recursive work or runtime. No change to the current fixed half/eighth campaign is justified by this review.
